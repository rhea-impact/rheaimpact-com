Who Neo Helps

Three groups. One mission.

👵

Seniors aging in place

Margaret is 78. She wants to stay in her home, not move to assisted living. Neo handles the cleaning, organizing, and daily tasks that have become barriers. She keeps her independence. Her family keeps their peace of mind.

👨‍👧

Single parents

David works two jobs. By the time he gets home, there's no energy left for his daughter—just dishes and laundry. Neo handles the house. David gets his evenings back.

🦽

People with mobility limitations

James served three tours. A roadside bomb changed everything. Neo doesn't give him back what he lost—but it restores capability. Independence. Dignity.

The most advanced robots in the world are being built for people who need them least. Tech billionaires buying $200,000 humanoids as status symbols while a widow in Fort Worth struggles to wash her own dishes.

We're changing that equation. Rhea Impact acquires humanoid robots and deploys them to households across Dallas-Fort Worth—not as products to sell, but as shared resources. A volunteer network transports them, sets them up, troubleshoots issues. Between deployments, volunteers get to use the robots themselves.

"The best time to figure out how to democratize this technology is before it's everywhere—not after."
— Daniel Shanklin, Founder

The 1X Neo navigates homes, manipulates objects, and learns household routines—bringing science fiction into everyday life.

This is an experiment. We don't know exactly how it will unfold. What we do know: the only way to answer the important questions is to start. To place the first robot, learn from what happens, and iterate.

If the model works in DFW, it can work anywhere.
